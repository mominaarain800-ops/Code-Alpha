Think Before You Click

Phishing Awareness Training: learn how to recognize suspicious messages, protect accounts, and report possible scams.

1. What is phishing?

Phishing is a social-engineering attack in which a message or website impersonates a trusted person or organization to trick someone into revealing information, opening a harmful attachment, or taking an unsafe action.

2. Common warning signs

  • Urgency or threats: “Your account will close in 10 minutes.”
  • Unexpected requests: asks for passwords, one-time codes, payment, or personal information.
  • Suspicious sender: the address is misspelled or does not match the real organization.
  • Unusual links: the visible text and actual destination do not match. Check the domain carefully.
  • Unexpected attachments: especially files you were not expecting.
  • Too good to be true: prizes, refunds, jobs, or rewards that require an immediate click.

3. Example: suspicious message

Subject: URGENT! Your student account will be deleted

“We detected a problem. Open the link and enter your password and verification code immediately to keep your account.”

Why it is suspicious: it uses pressure, requests credentials and a verification code, and gives no safe way to verify the request independently.

Safe action: do not click the link or reply. Open the official website by typing its known address yourself, or contact the organization using a trusted contact method.

4. Social engineering tactics

  • Authority: pretending to be a manager, teacher, bank, or IT support.
  • Fear: threatening account closure or legal trouble.
  • Curiosity: using sensational subjects or unexpected documents.
  • Scarcity/reward: claiming a limited-time prize or opportunity.
  • Familiarity: impersonating a colleague or friend.

5. Best practices

  • Use multi-factor authentication (MFA) and never share one-time codes.
  • Use a password manager and unique passwords for different accounts.
  • Verify unusual payment or information requests through a separate trusted channel.
  • Keep your operating system, browser, and security software updated.
  • Report suspicious messages using your organization’s approved reporting process.
  • If you clicked a suspicious link, report it quickly, change affected passwords from a trusted device, and contact IT support.
